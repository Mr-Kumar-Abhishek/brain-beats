## 2. Clinical Context: Pertussis & Post-Infectious Cough

### A. The Three Stages of Pertussis (Whooping Cough)
1. **Catarrhal Stage (Weeks 1–2):** Mild upper respiratory symptoms, low-grade fever, and mild non-productive cough.
2. **Paroxysmal Stage (Weeks 2–6):** Severe repetitive coughing paroxysms (10–30 rapid staccato coughs) followed by a stridor-like inspiratory "whoop," cyanosis, and post-tussive vomiting.
3. **Convalescent Stage (Weeks 6–12+):** Gradual recovery, though paroxysmal bouts may recur with secondary viral colds ("100-day cough").

### B. Therapeutic Integration of 46 Hz

## 6. Safety & Clinical Considerations

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Medical Care for Whooping Cough:** Pertussis is a highly contagious bacterial infection that can be life-threatening in infants and young children (apnea, hypoxemia, encephalopathy). Suspected pertussis requires urgent pediatric or medical diagnosis, macrolide antibiotic therapy (e.g., azithromycin), and public health reporting. Sound therapy is purely supportive.

> [!WARNING]
> Immediate emergency care is required for stridor at rest, severe shortness of breath, blue discoloration of lips/skin (cyanosis), or persistent vomiting leading to dehydration.
