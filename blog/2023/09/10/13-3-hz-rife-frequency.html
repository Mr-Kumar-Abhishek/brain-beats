2. Clinical Targets: Multiple Sclerosis Myelin Sheath Repair & Motor Rehabilitation

A. The Challenge of Demyelination in Multiple Sclerosis

In Multiple Sclerosis, inflammatory attacks by autoreactive immune cells compromise the myelin sheath—the fatty protective coating insulating nerve axons in the brain, spinal cord, and optic nerves:

  1. Conduction Block & Signal Lag: Without intact myelin, electrical action potentials leak across denuded axolemma membranes, causing profound weakness, sensory numbness, paresis, and ataxia.
  2. Axonal Vulnerability: Naked axons deprived of glial metabolic support eventually undergo irreversible transaction and Wallerian degeneration, driving progressive clinical disability.
  3. 13.3 Hz Bio-Acoustic Strategy: 13.3 Hz delivers targeted SMR entrainment and micro-vibrational coherence to reinforce the cellular micro-environment, facilitating remyelination while dampening motor hyperexcitability.

B. Enhancing Motor Control & Reducing Spasticity

Elevating 13.3 Hz SMR power across the sensorimotor cortex provides distinct neuromuscular benefits:

  • Reduces paraspinal and limb spasticity by regulating gamma motor neuron excitability.
  • Improves proprioceptive feedback and fine motor coordination in the hands and legs.
  • Alleviates motor fatigue, enabling longer endurance during daily activities and physical therapy.
