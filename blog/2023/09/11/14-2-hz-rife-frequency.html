By entraining central motor pathways to a precise 14.2 Hz cadence, this frequency provides an acoustic and bio-energetic modality aimed at overcoming paresis, enhancing neuromuscular recruitment, and supporting endogenous remyelination along damaged axonal pathways.

1. Neuroacoustic & Biophysical Mechanisms of 14.2 Hz

Operating at 14.2 Hz, this frequency bridges sensorimotor stability with active motor execution and cellular repair:

  • Neurotrophic Factor Upregulation: Neuro-acoustic stimulation in the Low-Beta band has been associated with enhanced release of Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) and Ciliary Neurotrophic Factor (CNTF), which are critical for neuronal survival and oligodendrocyte health.

2. Clinical Targets: MS Motor Impairment, Paresis & Demyelination

A. The Pathophysiology of Demyelinating Motor Loss

In demyelinating conditions like Multiple Sclerosis, focal inflammatory lesions in the brain and spinal cord strip axons of their protective myelin coating:

  1. Signal Dispersion & Motor Paresis: When myelin is degraded, action potentials travel at sluggish, variable speeds or fail completely to traverse damaged segments. Patients experience heavy limbs, foot drop, loss of fine motor precision, and severe muscle weakness.
  2. Paranodal Disruption: Breakdown of the paranodal axo-glial junction allows voltage-gated potassium channels ($K_v1.1 / K_v1.2$) to diffuse into the nodal gap, creating a hyperpolarizing “current sink” that prevents nerve firing.
  3. 14.2 Hz Bio-Acoustic Strategy: 14.2 Hz delivers resonant low-beta pulsing that stimulates motor neuron excitability, helps restore nodal electrical organization, and coordinates descending motor commands.

B. Synergy with Physical Rehabilitation & Gait Training

Integrating 14.2 Hz auditory stimulation into physical and occupational therapy sessions provides distinct rehabilitation advantages:

  • Promotes motor learning and neuro-plastic re-mapping around central lesions.
  • Enhances muscle activation efficiency, allowing patients to complete repetitive motor tasks with less premature fatigue.
  • Improves proprioception and postural stability during ambulation and balance exercises.
