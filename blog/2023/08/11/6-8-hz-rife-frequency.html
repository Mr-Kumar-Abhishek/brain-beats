2. Clinical Target: Muscle Spasms, Relaxation & Myofascial Release

A. The Biophysics of Skeletal Muscle Spasms & Myofascial Trigger Points

Musculoskeletal spasms and chronic muscle tightness develop through persistent neuromuscular feedback loops:

  1. Sustained Contraction & Ischemia: When muscle fibers contract involuntarily due to physical overexertion, injury, or emotional stress, local blood vessels are compressed, creating localized tissue hypoxia (ischemia).
  2. Metabolic Crisis: Ischemic tissue accumulates acidic metabolic byproducts (lactic acid, substance P, bradykinin), triggering nociceptors (pain receptors) that reflexively stimulate motor neurons to contract even harder.
  3. Calcium Entrapment: Impaired ATP synthesis prevents the sarcoplasmic reticulum from re-sequestering intracellular calcium ($Ca^{2+}$), locking actin-myosin cross-bridges in persistent rigidity.
